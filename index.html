How well the model matches your meter

Average hourly kWh for the whole house on 34 hot August 2026 days (bars), against the model running your schedule (74°F from 7 am to 7 pm on both floors). The model uses the old downstairs unit here, because that's what was installed in August. The 7 am drop and 7 pm spike in your meter show both thermostats setting back and recovering together.

MeterModel
  • Upstairs: a two-mass thermal model (air and structure) gaining heat through walls and windows, the attic ceiling, west sun and internal loads. It's calibrated to vent-sensor run times on 35 sunny August days: about 8-minute median runs and 11–12 hours of run time a day.
  • Downstairs: same structure, fit so that non-AC load plus upstairs plus downstairs (old unit) matches the meter hour by hour. The fit gives a much heavier thermal mass and much less attic exposure than upstairs, which is what you'd expect from a ground floor with a second story above it. The downstairs vent sensor couldn't be used directly: the meter shows its cold-air periods add less power than continuous compressor running would, so it can't be read as compressor run time.
  • Non-AC load: the winter 2026 hourly pattern (gas heat, in-laws living there), scaled to fit. It comes out around kWh/day.
  • Equipment (typical values, not measured): old upstairs 5-ton about 5.2 kW at 95°F. Old downstairs 30-year unit about 6.0 kW. New two-stage 5-ton about 4.3 kW on high stage and about 58% of that on low stage at 68% capacity, moving to high stage after 20 minutes or when it falls 0.6°F behind. Power rises about 0.9% and capacity falls about 0.6% per °F of outdoor heat. Variable speed is a rough estimate based on a typical inverter curve.
  • Dehumidifying hours is a proxy for moisture removal: run time after the first 5 minutes of each run, when the coil is cold enough to pull water. It's meant for comparing schedules, not as a humidity reading.
  • The floors are modeled separately. Heat moving between floors is absorbed into the calibration, which is reasonable because both thermostats follow the same schedule.
